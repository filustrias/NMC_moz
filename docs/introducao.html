MATAPA Project

Model of Care with Teamwork, Access, Participation and Coordination

Este documento foi criado para organizar as etapas e tarefas proposta para condução nosso projeto para a criação de um Novo Modelo de Cuidado em Moçambique.

THE PROBLEM

Primary Health Care (PHC) in Mozambique is in urgent need of modernization. Currently, services operate in a fragmented manner: vertical programs — HIV, tuberculosis, malaria, maternal and child health — run in isolation, with no integration between them. A patient with multiple health needs must attend different services, on different days, with different providers. This fragmentation is extremely inefficient: it overwhelms patients and services, leads to missed opportunities for preventive care, and makes continuity of care impossible.

THE PROPOSAL

The MATAPA Project aims to transform Mozambican PHC from a fragmented, episodic Model of Care into a New Model of Care (NMC) that is comprehensive, longitudinal, and coordinated with other levels of care. This NMC will be organized around multidisciplinary teams responsible for a defined panel of patients, capable of delivering comprehensive services centered on people’s needs.

METHODOLOGY

The project adopts Participatory Action Research as its methodological approach, structured in five iterative stages: (1) Participatory diagnosis (0–3 months), mapping existing fragmentation and barriers; (2) Co-design of the New Model of Care (3–6 months), translating the diagnosis into feasible and culturally appropriate solutions; (3) Assisted implementation in pilot units (6–18 months), with progressive expansion starting from units with the greatest readiness; (4) Continuous quarterly reflection and adjustment; and (5) Consolidation and institutionalization (18–24 months).

The project will be operationalized through three parallel axes:

  1. Structure & Digital: integrated records via KoBoToolbox, integrated service portfolio, and formative supervision.
  2. Teaching & Capacity Building: on-the-job mentoring, continuing education, and PDSA improvement cycles.
  3. Implementation, Monitoring and Evaluation: quantitative and qualitative indicators, performance dashboards, and monthly quality meetings.

THEORETICAL FRAMEWORK

The project adopts Participatory Action Research (PAR) as its central methodological approach. PAR is a research methodology in which local stakeholders — health professionals, managers, community leaders, and patients — actively participate in every stage of the process, from problem identification to the evaluation of solutions. This approach is a natural fit for MATAPA because the most sustainable transformations in health services emerge when solutions are co-constructed with those who experience them daily, rather than imposed top-down. The project further integrates internationally recognized frameworks: the 5S-5M-5C framework for analyzing PHC structure, processes, and outcomes; RE-AIM and CFIR for rigorous implementation evaluation; the Quadruple Aim as an outcomes framework (patient experience, team experience, population health, and efficiency); and Barbara Starfield’s essential PHC attributes (access, continuity, comprehensiveness, and coordination).

EXPECTED RESULTS (12–24 MONTHS)

The project aims to achieve the following results across 10 pilot health units in the municipality and province of Maputo: Design and implementation of the New Model of Care: co-construction of an NMC with Mozambican stakeholders, feasible and suited to the local context, including the definition of the service portfolio the NMC can deliver — and a proposal for its expansion — compared to the current Model of Care. Patient enrollment and longitudinal follow-up: registration of patients and families (empanelment), with ongoing follow-up by family physicians and their health teams, identification of the main morbidities in the enrolled population, and longitudinal management of patients and their various health conditions. Monitoring and evaluation: development of individualized monitoring indicators based on each patient’s morbidity profile; identification of the population within key care pathways (maternal and child health, HIV, tuberculosis, malnutrition, malaria, non-communicable diseases); and comparison of care pathway indicators between the enrolled and non-enrolled populations.
